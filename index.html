Customer 360 Behavioral Analytics

Analysis of how TV customers watch and search, from raw logs to Power BI.

Dec 2025Personal projectData AnalystData Engineer

Download PDFPDF Live dashboardDashboard Source codeCode
Telecom Customer 360Overview

A study of how telecom customers watch and search for content, built from raw logs through to two Power BI pages.

Outside the selected path.

The problem
A content team needs to know how often customers come back, what they spend time watching, and whether their interests change from one month to the next. The raw logs record only individual events.
What I did
  • Built a PySpark pipeline that turns 48.5 million viewing records into one behavior profile per contract.
  • Ranked each user’s top search keyword with window functions and labeled its genre in batches with GPT-4o-mini.
  • Published both result tables to MySQL and built the Power BI report on top.
How data moves
  1. JSON and Parquet logs
  2. PySpark
  3. Genre labeling
  4. MySQL
  5. Power BI
The result
Television is the most-watched category for almost every contract, yet search interest is fluid: about 69% of users present in both periods changed their main genre between June and July 2022. That points the content team to specific moves worth testing, such as suggesting Chinese drama to drama searchers.

01Recruitment Data Platform

A streaming pipeline that turns job-ad clicks and applications into hourly KPIs.

May 2026Personal projectData Engineer

Download PDFPDF Source codeCode
Recruitment Data PlatformOverview
Next projectRetail Banking Lakehouse

A streaming and batch pipeline that turns individual clicks and applications into hourly numbers for each job and channel.

Outside the selected path.

The problem
One job ad runs on several channels. To compare them, a recruiting team would have to regroup and recount raw events by job, channel and hour every time it needs a report.
What I did
  • Streamed synthetic events from Kafka with Spark, using an explicit schema and checkpoints.
  • Stored every event in Cassandra, then processed only the new ones with a high-watermark batch job.
  • Joined job and company data and scheduled the job every minute in Airflow.
How data moves
  1. Kafka
  2. Spark streaming
  3. Cassandra
  4. Airflow and PySpark
  5. MySQL
The result
A reporting table that updates itself with six hourly measures, from clicks and conversions to qualified applications and spend, ready for a BI tool.

02Retail Banking Customer 360 Lakehouse

A data lakehouse that builds one privacy-safe profile of each bank customer.

Jul 2026Personal projectData Engineer

Download PDFPDF Source codeCode
Retail Banking LakehouseOverview
Previous projectRecruitment Data Platform

A lakehouse that gives a retail bank one trustworthy, privacy-safe view of each customer, rebuilt every day.

Outside the selected path.

The problem
Customer data is split across core banking, card and CRM systems. Marketing waits for manual extracts, gets different answers each time, and risks seeing personal data it should not.
What I did
  • Modeled ten source tables, over 1.3 million synthetic records, into Bronze, Silver and Gold layers.
  • Kept history for customers, accounts, products and branches with SCD Type 2, safe to re-run.
  • Added quality gates, masked personal data and limited Marketing to the sandbox through Trino roles.
How data moves
  1. Oracle and PostgreSQL
  2. Bronze
  3. Silver
  4. Gold
  5. Masked sandbox
  6. Trino and Superset
The result
The acceptance run passed all 19 quality checks. Segmentation, churn, cross-sell and next-best-offer questions became self-service SQL on masked tables, with no raw personal data exposed.

01Hotel Digital Transformation

Revenue analysis, pricing proposal and front-desk system for a hotel.

Eight-week planClient projectData Analyst

Download PDFPDF Live demoDemo
Hotel Digital TransformationOverview
Next projectKalotoys WMS

Finding out why a hotel missed its revenue target, then turning the answer into a pricing proposal, a front-desk system and management reporting.

Outside the selected path.

The problem
A hotel in Ho Chi Minh City was missing its revenue target, while staff believed they had met theirs. Operations and revenue were written on paper, and bookings from OTA, offline and travel agency channels were not managed in one place.
The goal
Find out why revenue fell short, then centralize booking data so that changes can be traced and revenue reconciled. The hotel set a 20% revenue increase as its condition for continuing the partnership.
The plan
  • Weeks 1–2: agree on KPI definitions, then digitize and standardize the records.
  • Weeks 3–4: confirm requirements, design and develop a trial system.
  • Weeks 5–8: pilot, train, adjust, evaluate and hand over.
What the data showed

All figures and charts below use demo data.

  • From June to October 2024, occupancy averaged 46.22% and fell to 17.83% in October. Low occupancy was the main cause of the shortfall.
  • Standard and Deluxe rooms made up 67.53% of nights sold and 66.84% of room revenue.
  • Staff bookings were 70.63% of the total. That explains why staff felt on target while rooms stayed empty.
  • Collaborator bookings were priced only about 2.4% above OTA bookings.
The proposal
Raise offline and direct rates by 20%, since that group books most rooms and was assessed as less price-sensitive. Keep online and OTA rates unchanged to stay competitive and help fill rooms.
Technology choices
  • Azure over Supabase: one source for operations and BI, role-based access and room to add OTA or PMS connections.
  • Power BI over Looker: works with the existing Excel files and Azure SQL, and can be adopted sooner.
The solution
  • A relational model of five tables, with each booking linked to a guest, room and channel, and totals calculated in the database.
  • A front-desk app for reservations, check-in, early checkout, payments, debt and invoices.
  • Room calendars by day and hour, weekday rate tables and maintenance schedules.
  • Two Power BI pages inside the app: business overview and room monitoring.
How data moves
  1. Front desk
  2. Next.js
  3. ASP.NET Core API
  4. Azure SQL
  5. Power BI
Where it stands
The system is deployed with demo data. The 20% revenue increase is a target, to be judged against a comparable period with seasonality taken into account.

02Kalotoys Warehouse Management System

An inventory system and dashboard for a wooden-toy maker.

Oct–Nov 2026, four-week planClient projectData AnalystData Engineer

Download PDFPDF Live demoDemo
Kalotoys WMSOverview
Previous projectHotel Digital Transformation

A warehouse system that tells a wooden-toy maker what material it has, where it sits and when to buy more.

Outside the selected path.

The problem
Kalotoys makes wooden educational toys, including ones personalized with a child’s name. Material was tracked in Excel, withdrawals were not recorded consistently and shortages were reported late, so production waited and orders piled up.
The goal
Answer three questions for every material: how much is there, how much was used and how much is left. Average daily use then shows how many days of production the stock covers, so purchasing can act early.
The plan
  • Week 1: survey the warehouse and standardize codes, units and specifications.
  • Weeks 2–3: design the database, build the app, then run a trial and guide users.
  • Week 4: acceptance and handover.
The data design
  • A sample flat-file ledger with eight transaction types, one row per material movement in one slot.
  • Five MongoDB collections that keep the movement ledger separate from current balances.
  • One balance per material and slot. Each movement and its balance update are written in the same transaction.
  • A transfer is two paired rows, so total stock never changes when material moves.
Technology choices
  • MongoDB Atlas over Supabase and Azure SQL: a direct fit with the Node.js backend at low starting cost.
  • Power BI over Looker Studio: Power Query, DAX and a .pbix file that can be handed over.
  • Vercel over Render and Azure App Service: the least effort to release and maintain a Next.js app.
The solution
  • Forms for receipts, issues to the workshop, returns, transfers and adjustments, with the balance checked before issuing.
  • Stock lookup by slot, a rack map, low-stock alerts and export to Excel.
  • Transaction history and an opening, in, out and closing report for reconciliation.
How data moves
  1. Stock movement
  2. Next.js
  3. MongoDB Atlas
  4. Fabric Warehouse
  5. Power BI
What the reports reveal
In the sample data at the 10 November 2026 cut-off, 28 slots were in use, six materials were below their threshold and seven locations had held stock for over 45 days. A manager can see what to buy and which shelf to check.
Where it stands
A working trial version is online with sample data, covering the catalog, warehouse operations, alerts and reporting.
